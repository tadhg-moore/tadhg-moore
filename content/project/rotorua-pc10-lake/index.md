**Client:** Bay of Plenty Regional Council

To support Plan Change 10 (PC10) to the Bay of Plenty Regional Water and Land Plan — which sets a nitrogen reduction target for Lake Rotorua — Limnotrack is upgrading the Lake Rotorua hydrodynamic-ecological model to [GLM-AED](https://aquatic.science.uwa.edu.au/research/models/AED/), an open-source model that better represents vertical mixing, sediment–water nutrient feedback, and management interventions under variable climate conditions.

A key feature of the upgrade is a mechanistic representation of alum–phosphorus interactions, developed in collaboration with the University of Western Australia, simulating the hydrolysis and speciation of dosed alum, pH- and temperature-dependent phosphate binding, and sediment-mediated sorption — replacing the current daily alum dosing regime with a process-based scenario tool. Once calibrated, the model will be used to generate long-term climate change and management scenarios, including preliminary advice on potential downstream nutrient impacts to Lake Rotoiti.

This lake modelling work runs alongside, but independently of, the companion [Rotorua PC10 catchment modelling](../rotorua-pc10-catchment/) workstream.
